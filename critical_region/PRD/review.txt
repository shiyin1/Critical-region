Report of the Referee -- DQ14414/Yin
----------------------------------------------------------------------

In this paper, the author investigates the extent of the critical scaling region of the chiral phase transition of strongly interacting matter at finite density, in the chiral limit. To this end, a two-flavor quark-meson model is studied within the functional renormalization group framework, in both the local potential approximation (LPA) and its LPA′ extension. The author extracts the critical exponents of the transition and quantifies the range over which the corresponding scaling relations remain valid. The main result is that the scaling region shrinks with increasing chemical potential in both truncations, with LPA′ yielding a quantitatively smaller region than LPA.

The paper is interesting and contains a meaningful contribution to understanding the QCD phase diagram. Before I can recommend publication, however, I would like the author to address the following points.

1) The author employs the two-flavor quark-meson model in the chiral limit. Given that the real-world QCD phase transition is more plausibly governed by the dynamics of three light flavors (or at least is sensitive to the strange quark sector through the axial anomaly), what is the motivation for restricting the analysis to N_f = 2 ? A brief justification, even if purely practical, would help the reader assess the relevance of the results for the physical Columbia plot.

2) The discussion at the beginning of Sec. II, and Sec. III B in particular, is central to the method but is currently too brief. The author notes that critical exponents are conventionally obtained from RG fixed points, but omits that this is normally done in the dimensionally-reduced, d=3 theory, in which temperature does not appear explicitly. The author should clarify why, and in what precise sense, the 3+1-dimensional finite-temperature calculation used here reproduces the same critical behavior, by including more detail on the relevant rescalings, the role of temperature in them, and the correspondence to the standard RG fixed-point treatment.

3) The minimal pion mass reached is 2.7*10^−4 MeV, with the explicit symmetry-breaking term tuned to be "small enough" rather than strictly zero. Can the author quantify how the extracted critical exponents and the size of the scaling plateau depend on the value of c used?

4) Is there a specific reason why the flow of the dimensionful Yukawa coupling is neglected? Can the author estimate the error introduced by this approximation?

5) In the right-hand panels of Figs. 5 and 6, there appear to be relatively few data points above μ_B=300 MeV. How numerically costly would it be to compute additional points so the curves become smoother? This would also help in seeing the location of the maxima more precisely.

6) The summary does not provide the reader with quantitative remarks characterizing the critical region. The author should include concrete numbers (e.g., approximate widths in T or m_pi at some values of mu_B) so the conclusions are more quantitatively grounded.

7) The author may want to comment in the conclusions on whether the relatively rapid shrinkage of the critical region at high mu_B found in the QM model is expected to over- or underestimate the true size of the QCD critical region.
